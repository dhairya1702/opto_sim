# Instrument interaction update

Krimsky is now available, bringing the active library to 20 modules. It requires baseline corneal-reflex inspection, fixation, a 50 cm light and monocular examiner viewing before prism neutralisation. Standard placement is before deviating OS; modified placement is before fixating OD, following the supplied source's naming. Authored left exo (20Δ BI) and eso (15Δ BO) examples show changing reflex asymmetry, including overcorrection and incorrect-base divergence. The learner places the prism on the patient view or with left/right keys, rotates its base with the control or B key, and adjusts power by dragging, up/down keys, or the slider. Record power/base/eye/method only at matching relative reflex positions. This is a draft illustrative model with visible reflexes, not a scarred-cornea simulation.

The guided Practice shell groups its 20 modules by motor alignment, sensory status, latent deviation, vergence, and accommodation. It shows session progress, marks a completed module once, and offers the next guided module after completion. Completion controls lock into a review state until the learner explicitly resets or starts a new patient pattern. Worth target distance is now controlled directly in the patient view by drag, wheel, or arrow keys, with the slider and near/distance buttons retained as accessible alternatives.

Four-prism base-out now has a dedicated patient scene with draggable prism placement, selectable prism power/base, and timed two-phase eye movements. Normal placements show conjugate version followed by fellow-eye refixation. In the authored OS suppression case, OD placement produces version without refixation; OS placement produces no movement. OD is tested first, then OS, and each placement requires a steady observation hold. Moving away early cancels the current hold, while changing power/base resets all observation credit. Placement buttons and arrow keys provide alternatives to dragging. Timing and movement size are illustrative, not calibrated.

Modified Thorington now uses an interactive, enlarged numbered card diagram. The learner drags the penlight spot to the central hole, sets 40 cm, seats the Maddox rod before OD, and rotates its grooves for horizontal or vertical readings. Asking the patient reveals the streak and reported number. Both axes must be recorded; new cases reverse the direction. Setup changes clear the current observation. Arrow keys and centre-light controls provide alternatives to dragging. The screen is explicitly a patient-view teaching diagram; realistic hand/card rendering and calibrated optics are not implemented in this version.

Maddox rod has a dedicated full-screen trainer with movable rod position, visible grooves, 90° rotation, and draggable prism power. The rod must align with OD and the correct axis; correction, fixation, light, and base direction gate the patient-percept diagram. Each measurement introduces 20Δ before reducing toward streak/spot coincidence. Setup changes invalidate the current neutralisation. Horizontal and vertical measurements are required at both 6 m and 40 cm. Native seating/rotation controls, arrow keys, R, and a range input provide alternatives to dragging. The existing fictional 6Δ BI and 4Δ BU endpoints are retained; the current trainer covers those neutralisation directions, not all phoria patterns.

Stereopsis now uses a clickable three-circle booklet with a patient-response workflow: present the next disparity set, wait for the scripted patient's selection, and mark the reported circle before advancing. Circle positions vary between sets. Two consecutive wrong patient responses stop presentation; recording requires the last correct threshold. The authored sequence remains correct through 100 arcsec, followed by errors at 60 and 40 arcsec. Moving the booklet clears the run; 40 cm and viewing-glasses setup are required. A separate, exaggerated patient-percept diagram illustrates depth without implying that a flat screen provides a calibrated stereo test. Native circle buttons support keyboard input.

Worth four dot now has a full-screen trainer with a clickable illuminated target, separate red/green filter checks, an illustrated patient-reported percept, target-distance dragging and keyboard-accessible controls. Dot count and interpretation must be recorded at 40 cm and 6 m; moving or switching the light clears the current response. One fictional scenario fuses at near and suppresses OS at distance; its 200 cm transition is authored teaching data, not a clinical cutoff or measured scotoma size. Other patterns include persistent suppression and horizontal/vertical diplopia. The percept is enlarged for readability and is not optically calibrated.

Accommodative facility now uses a clickable, rotating ±2.00 D flipper, a near card fixed at 40 cm, frame-mounted fellow-eye occlusion for OD/OS, and both eyes open for OU. Each run starts with plus and lasts 60 seconds measured from elapsed time. The fictional patient must clear each side before another flip is accepted; only a complete plus/minus pair earns one cycle. Results are recorded separately for OD, OS, and OU. Reset cancels the current timer and clears that run; Space and native buttons offer keyboard alternatives. Reduced-motion settings remove the flipping transition. Scripted clearing delays are illustrative rather than clinical norms.

NRA/PRA uses a clickable binocular lens pair with 0.25 D changes and fictional patient-response pauses. NRA is saved at the authored +2.00 D blur endpoint, then plus power must be removed step by step to baseline and clear vision confirmed before PRA begins. PRA retains its authored −2.25 D endpoint. Both signed results remain visible. The target remains at 40 cm, both eyes stay open, and Space or labeled buttons provide alternatives to clicking the scene. This scenario uses a non-presbyopic patient with distance correction.

Minus-lens amplitude has a dedicated full-screen trainer. A clickable red trial lens moves into the frame after each 0.25 D change; buttons and minus/plus keys provide equivalent input. Frame-mounted fellow-eye occlusion frees the second hand to hold the target at 40 cm. A fictional 1.2-second response delay prevents repeated input while the patient tries to clear the letters. First sustained blur remains authored at −4.00 D, giving 6.50 D including the 2.50 D working-distance demand. OD recording resets lens power and fixation/occlusion setup for OS; both recordings are required. The examiner's card stays sharp; patient blur is conveyed through the response.

Practice now keeps its written teaching section and separates the attempt into a full-screen first-person clinical stage. Bruckner and Hirschberg show a procedural 3D patient, a compact examiner-side instrument hand, and a visible light cone that follows pointer or keyboard aim without obscuring the patient's face. Distance can be changed by wheel, keyboard, slider, or touch controls. Bruckner uses the off hand only while selecting the aperture, then removes it from the scene. Its large-spot footprint visibly covers both pupils and the revealed red reflexes use larger, high-contrast centres and halos so equal and unequal examples can be compared. Examiner alignment is now a direct drag or arrow-key task on a two-dimensional alignment pad; moving the pad also shifts the simulated viewing position. Fixation instruction, illumination, working distance, aim, and examiner viewing alignment gate the simulated reflex, so the answer cannot be read before technique is established. Extraocular motility now shows the held penlight and both-hand posture and requires the penlight to be on before gaze-position dwell counts. Every hands-on examination stage now occupies the entire viewport on desktop and mobile; only information and confirmation dialogs remain compact.

Cover–uncover and alternating-cover Practice modules now use the same full-screen interaction model. The dominant hand moves a 3D occluder; the other hand holds a 40 cm near target or prism bar when required. Cover–uncover varies orthophoria, unilateral esotropia, and exophoria so the learner must distinguish movement while the fellow eye is covered from movement immediately on uncovering. Alternating cover requires direct eye-to-eye shifts without an uncovered interval, maps refixation direction to deviation, and makes the learner choose prism base and increase prism power before repeating the sequence. Movement disappears only at the authored neutralising prism endpoint.

Sensory-status Practice now includes Worth four dot, stereopsis, and the 4Δ base-out test. Worth practice requires habitual correction, correctly oriented red–green glasses, colour-cancellation checking, distance selection, and interpretation of dot count/colour/position. Stereopsis requires near correction, viewing glasses, a 40 cm booklet, progression through disparity levels, and the last-correct endpoint before two consecutive errors. The four-prism module requires best correction, an isolated distance target, exactly 4Δ base out, placement before both eyes, and comparison of the outward version and inward refixation phases.

Latent-deviation Practice now includes Maddox rod and Modified Thorington. Both require correction, a Maddox rod before OD, axis-specific groove orientation, and a lit target. Maddox practice supports distance and near, starts with base-in or base-up prism, and changes the patient-visible streak as prism is reduced to coincidence. Modified Thorington fixes the card at 40 cm and records the numbered streak position as prism-dioptre magnitude and phoria direction.

Vergence Practice now includes NPC, horizontal distance, vertical distance, horizontal near, and near vergence facility. Full-screen stages record subjective and objective NPC break/recovery separately, require blur before break where a blur endpoint exists, preserve `X` for distance BI and vertical blur, enforce BI before BO and BU before BD, and use a real 60-second 12Δ BO / 3Δ BI facility run. Setup changes invalidate observations made under the old configuration.

Accommodation Practice now includes push-up amplitude, minus-lens amplitude, NRA/PRA, and accommodative facility. Push-up requires OD, OS, and OU endpoints; minus-lens requires both monocular endpoints; and facility requires separate 60-second OD, OS, and OU runs. NRA is retained visibly while the lenses return to baseline for PRA. Eye changes are disabled during timed runs, and completion is unavailable until every required eye has a recorded result.

The NPC trainer is the first vergence module converted from slider-first controls to direct first-person handling. The learner grips and moves an accommodative target through simulated depth with pointer drag, wheel, touch, or arrow keys while the other hand measures from the spectacle plane. The patient eyes visibly converge, one eye loses fixation at the objective break, and alignment returns during recovery. A labeled range input remains as the accessible alternative.

Horizontal fusional vergence at distance now uses a directly handled prism bar. The learner places it before OD, completes BI before BO, drags the bar upward through increasing prism powers, records the applicable blur/break endpoint, reverses direction, and drags down to recovery. Prism orientation, the selected prism aperture, the examiner hand, compensating eye movements, loss of fusion, and recovery are visible in the patient scene. Wheel and arrow input plus a labeled range remain available.

Horizontal fusional vergence at near uses the same direct prism-bar handling with the near-specific binocular demand. The non-dominant hand holds a visible accommodative card at 40 cm while the dominant hand moves the BI/BO prism bar. Both near blur endpoints, break, and recovery must be recorded in order, and the patient eyes begin from a converged near-fixation posture.

Vertical fusional vergence at distance now uses the directly handled prism bar as well. The triangular prism orientation visibly changes between BU and BD, the simulated eyes make opposing vertical vergence movements, and loss of fusion persists until the learner reverses the bar to the recovery endpoint. Only break and recovery are recorded because vertical vergence has no blur endpoint.

Near fusional vergence facility now uses a directly rotated two-sided flipper rather than a generic cycle button. The dominant hand alternates 12Δ BO and 3Δ BI while the non-dominant hand holds the 40 cm card. Each side has a short visible clearing interval; input is accepted only after the patient reports clear and single, and a cycle is counted only after both sides. The run lasts 60 seconds and supports clicking the flipper or pressing Space.

Accommodation push-up now uses a directly moved near card. The learner grips the card and moves it toward the spectacle plane with drag, wheel, touch, or arrow input; the simulated print visibly loses clarity at first sustained blur. OD and OS runs show the fellow eye physically occluded, while OU shows convergence and assigns the free hand to measurement. The scene displays distance and calculated dioptric amplitude, and all OD, OS, and OU endpoints remain required.

The interview is history-only: the General examinations shortcut is removed. Acquired history continues to save automatically to Notes. Return to the room and choose equipment independently; the accessible station list remains available without recommending the next examination.

Eyes-only visual revision: the brown face sphere and raised skin blobs are removed from every examination view. `EyeSurface.tsx` uses one curved almond-shaped mesh with a procedural iris/pupil/sclera shader, avoiding the former intersecting iris plane and white eyeball. Pupil response, gaze following, tear-film highlights and thin lid margins remain illustrative. OD/OS shows the selected eye; OU shows the pair. The slit beam follows the pointer. Acuity shows a labeled simulated chart alongside the eye. Fundus now has a separate explicitly schematic posterior-pole view in `PosteriorPole.tsx`; its appearance is not validated diagnostic imagery and does not add findings or peripheral-retina coverage. These original code-generated visual assets share the existing project CC0 visual-asset dedication.

Return-flow correction: completing an examination now uses **Record finding & return to room**, an explicit user action that closes the close-up before requesting mouse capture. It returns to the collision-free patient viewpoint. Putting an instrument down also offers an explicit return. Escape/cancel never silently recaptures; a visible **Return to room · enable mouse-look** control allows retry even after a previous rejection. Drag and held-key state clear on close-up transitions, and implausibly large pointer-lock transition deltas are discarded. Finding notifications now derive from recorded exam/repeat events for every procedure and remain for nine seconds.

Latest update: Aim the crosshair at an individual trolley instrument and press E (or click it) to pick it up directly. The trolley surface no longer opens a kit through the 3D ray. Eye/configuration controls are available while holding it. Point at the patient and press E to examine. The station list remains an alternate route.

In the close-up, mouse movement, touch movement or arrow keys position the instrument. The torch light follows that position, with a non-calibrated illustrative pupil response. No precision movement is scored. Recording returns to the room, retains the instrument, selects OS after OD where applicable, and displays the finding in a fading notification for nine seconds. The notebook retains the result. Close-up iris fibres are deterministic procedural shader detail, with thin lid margins and tear-film reflections; original project visual-asset licensing applies.

The opening card is centered and titled “Your patient”. Layout adjustments separate its actions and wrap compact controls.

Select a room instrument (or station), choose the eye/procedure, and press **Pick up instrument & examine**. A held instrument lifts into view. Click Arun or **Examine Arun** to open a short animated 3D examination close-up. Use **Record finding** to commit the case-authored result; cancelling records nothing. The slit lamp uses its fixed station and has no pickup.

The pen torch illustrates a pupil-light response. Other views include an occluder sweep, motility target, trial frame, instrument positioning and a slit-light illustration. OD/OS selects the zoomed eye. Retinoscopy now includes a fictional spherical reflex model with with/neutral/against motion and two-meridian confirmation; it remains an educational approximation rather than validated optics or technique assessment. Ophthalmoscopy shows the schematic posterior pole described above. All clinical reference findings continue to come from the existing case engine.

The close-up and held equipment are original procedural geometry in `web/scene/ExaminationView.tsx`, under the same visual-asset CC0 dedication as the existing project models. No external assets or dependencies were added.

Verification: TypeScript and production build, existing domain tests. Browser automation was not run for this update, as requested. Earlier end-to-end scripts reference the previous immediate-examination button and need updating before reuse.
