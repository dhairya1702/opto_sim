In immersive consultation they dispatch to the active scene, which returns a context-grounded acknowledgement or clarification and changes only the supported patient behavior; the voice layer never writes an observation, completes a procedure, or invents a finding. In the open Test interview panel, a symptom question remains history rather than being mistaken for an examination instruction. The browser reports successful cached playback so the server does not issue duplicate TTS. Unmatched conversation may use Gemini Live, but if live output does not begin within two seconds the server routes the prerecorded uncertainty reply, making silence an invalid completed turn. Content-free logs distinguish unmatched turns, Gemini output start, and timeout fallback. Runtime TTS remains a fallback for dynamic grounded responses and missing assets. Barge-in and the canonical-output arbiter still stop stale or competing playback.

Voice controls appear only after the learner enters an active desktop attempt or the VR launch view; the mode selector, Practice library, lesson overview, and Test briefing do not request or expose microphone access. Immersive views add only a small wall status sign; the existing B/Y utility panel remains unchanged. Leaving an active Practice attempt, muting, completing Test, or exiting a mode releases its microphone, audio graph, socket, and playback. Typed and controller controls always remain available. This behavior remains disabled by default pending hosted and physical-device acceptance. See `docs/gcp-hosting-voice.md`.

The canonical patient model used by Test and the shared Practice XR clinic now adds short randomized blinks and procedural mouth movement while patient audio playback is active. Mouth movement closes smoothly on playback completion or interruption and becomes a steady speaking pose under reduced-motion preference. Facial overlays do not participate in raycasting and do not move the consultation gaze/pupil nodes, alter authored findings, or affect technique gates.

Extraocular-motility Guided VR now has the first dedicated next-step coach. A soft warm card on the rear wall uses separate, larger title and instruction regions to show one of six steps: pick up the penlight, instruct the patient, establish illuminated primary gaze at 30–40 cm, complete all nine H-pattern positions, ask about symptoms, and explicitly record the observation. The first step is also visible in clinic-layout preview so placement can be checked without entering immersive VR. The bubble advances only from the existing tool, instruction, dwell, symptom and submission state; it does not perform or record a step. It is non-interactive, does not intercept controller rays, and is absent from independent Test attempts. When present, it suppresses the generic arrival placard and transient wall handling message to prevent overlapping content. The shared room no longer carries the decorative consultation-number sign. The existing gaze guides and fitted findings board remain separate technique and recording surfaces.

Motility coach follow-up: the guide now keys primary-gaze completion to the canonical `centre` checkpoint used by the procedure rather than the display wording “primary.” A completed spoken symptom question returns the fixed fictional response “No double vision, pain, or discomfort in any direction” through its own canonical audio key, instead of scraping the changing board status for runtime speech.

Paddle and cover-coach follow-up: the acuity occluder, pinhole, and cover occluder now follow the controller aiming (target-ray) pose while held, matching the prism bar and scopes. Previously they followed the Quest grip pose, whose tilt laid the handle back along the forearm instead of holding the paddle upright in front of the patient. Guided cover–uncover and alternating-cover step 3 now names the next required occluder position (for example “Move 1 of 4: Cover OD · watch OS”), shows progress, and surfaces restart feedback instead of referring to an order that was not visible in the headset. The coach still only reflects the existing ordered-sequence state; it does not advance or record anything.

Cover-test placement guide: Guided Practice for cover–uncover and alternating cover now shows a single motility-style ring while the cover paddle is held, with no text near it. Only the current move is drawn: for a cover step, one gold ring in front of the required eye that matches the accepted paddle zone; for an uncover step, one ring to the side of the eye just uncovered. The ring turns green with a hold-progress arc when the paddle is in place. The step wording stays in the rear-wall guided bubble. Ring positions come from the same tolerances the sequence uses (`xrCoverGuideAnchors` in `xrPracticeBatch.ts`). The guide ignores controller rays and never advances, credits, or records. It also appears during alternating-cover prism repeats, can be hidden with SHOW/HIDE COVER GUIDE in the A/X menu, and is absent from independent Test attempts.

Cover-test eye movement: the XR cover lessons replace the fixed snap-and-return pulse with sustained eye positions that follow the physical occluder (`web/interaction/coverDeviation.ts`). A tropia is visible before covering, refixation holds while covered, phoric drift and recovery are slower than refixation, and iris shift scales with the Δ amount relative to the rendered iris. In alternating cover, prism held before an eye reduces the visible movement by its power. Sequence order, dwell, recording gates, and neutralising endpoints are unchanged. The desktop trainer (`CoverPracticeStage.tsx`) now uses the same model, scaled to its own drawn iris (`EYE_SURFACE_IRIS_RADIUS`). From the first “Repeat alternate cover with prism”, the selected prism stays before the eye and changes to base or power take effect immediately, until the sequence is reset. A Playwright spec (`cover-practice.spec.ts`) runs the desktop cover–uncover attempt with the keyboard.

Alternating-cover follow-up: the eyes are now linked. The covered eye moves with the refixation instead of drifting on every shift, vertical deviations move the eyes oppositely, and the eye behind a prism turns toward the apex. Neutralisation is computed from the model, with the prism before either eye. Recording asks for OD's movement on uncovering and an R/L-labelled deviation. Desktop changes: alternating cover restarts if the occluder is lowered clear of the face for more than 0.35 s (crossing the nose by keyboard is neither timed nor credited); the deviation is no longer auto-filled from the movement; and a required “Prism before OD/OS” choice moves the prism bar before that eye. Two input fixes came out of the new Playwright spec: arrow keys now use functional updates, so presses before a re-render are not lost; and the sequence timer's refs are no longer overwritten from render state.
